The Tanner Street municipal lot has 84 spaces. On a normal weekday, according to the city's own counts, about 31 of them are in use at the busiest hour. For six Saturdays this year it held the Tanner Street Market instead, and on the busiest of those days a volunteer with a clicker counted 2,300 people.

## How it happened

It started with a permit, not a plan. A group of growers wanted a place to sell that wasn't a church parking lot, and someone noticed the city lot sat mostly empty on weekends. A temporary use permit costs $85 and takes about three weeks. The market's organizers filed six of them.

The surprise was how little else had to change. The lot already had lights, drainage and a curb cut. The market brought its own tents. The only real cost was a portable toilet and a dumpster, which the vendors split.

## What the shops noticed

I talked to owners of the five shops facing the lot. Four said Saturday sales were up on market days, one by nearly half. The fifth, a tire shop, said it was quieter, but its owner also said he'd rather have the foot traffic than the empty lot. Nobody reported trouble finding parking, because most market visitors walked or came on the 14.

## Making it stick

Here's the part that matters for other neighborhoods. Six temporary permits are fragile: one complaint and next year's might not come through. A few cities have created a standing category for weekend use of municipal lots, so a group can apply once a year with a plan rather than six times with a hope.

Corliss's planning commission is reviewing its temporary use rules this winter. If you'd like a market, a play street or an outdoor movie in a lot near you, that review is the place to say so. Written comments count as much as speaking in person.
