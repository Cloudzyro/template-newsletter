The 22 bus is scheduled to cover the two miles of Alder Street between Ferry Road and the hospital in eleven minutes. Riders will tell you it never does. So over two weeks I rode it eleven times with a stopwatch and a notebook, and wrote down every second the bus wasn't moving.

## Where the time went

The average trip took sixteen minutes. Of the five extra minutes, only about one and a half came from cars in the way. The rest broke down like this, on average:

- Two minutes at red lights, mostly at four intersections where the bus arrives just as the signal changes
- One minute and ten seconds of people paying with cash at the front door
- Forty seconds pulling back into traffic after a stop

None of that is mysterious, and most of it is fixable without a single new lane.

## Signals that wait

Corliss already owns the equipment that lets a bus ask a traffic light to stay green a few seconds longer. It was installed on Alder in 2019. It was never switched on, because the two departments that would have to agree on the settings never scheduled the meeting. I am not making this up. I have the email thread.

## Paying at the back

Cities that let riders board at every door and tap a card on the way in save close to a minute per trip on busy routes. Corliss allows it on exactly one route, the downtown shuttle, as a pilot that ended four years ago and was never extended or cancelled.

## What would help

Five minutes doesn't sound like much. But the 22 runs every twelve minutes, and five minutes on every trip means the authority needs an extra bus just to keep that schedule. Switching on the signals and extending all-door boarding would pay for themselves. Ask your council member which department owns the Alder Street signal settings, and watch how long the answer takes.
